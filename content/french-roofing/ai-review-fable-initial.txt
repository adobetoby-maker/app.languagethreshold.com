4. **L3 words `sûr`, reading line 7.** `sûr` primarily reads as "sure, certain" at this level, so `je suis sûr` means "I'm sure" and `il est sûr` can read "he is sure". Suggested reading line 7: `Oui, le harnais est prêt et il est sans danger.` with a note listing `sécuritaire` for Quebec.

**Regional notes**

- Numbers: Quebec and France both use `soixante-dix`, `quatre-vingt-dix`. Belgium and Switzerland use `septante`, `nonante`. Worth one line in L2 grammarFocus.
- L2 opener `Chef,` as a vocative is common in France; Quebec crews say `Boss`.
- Typography: the space before `?` and inside « » is France style; Quebec usually omits the space before `?`.
- `Bonjour` covers all day in both; `Bon matin` is heard in Quebec but is a calque. Optional note on L1.
- `matériel` versus `équipement` for equipment: both understood, `équipement` more common in Quebec.
