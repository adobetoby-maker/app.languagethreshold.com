## Material findings

**1. L5: `le char` is wrong for "truck".** It appears in two places: `words[the truck].noteEnglish` and `regionalNotesEnglish[0]`. In Quebec, *char* means a car, not a truck. A learner would mislabel a vehicle on site.
- Word note, replace with: "Le camion is standard everywhere. In informal Quebec speech, a pickup is often le pick-up or le truck. Le char means a car, not a truck."
- `regionalNotesEnglish[0]`, replace with: "In Quebec, le char is a car. Pickups are often called le pick-up or le truck informally. Le camion is standard and understood everywhere."

**2. L6: the lunch-word warning is missing its key half.** This affects `words[the lunch].noteEnglish` and `regionalNotesEnglish[0]`. The draft teaches *le déjeuner* = lunch. It never says that in Quebec, Belgium and Switzerland, *le déjeuner* means breakfast. A Quebec crew would hear "breakfast."
- Word note, replace with: "France: le déjeuner = lunch. Quebec, Belgium and Switzerland: le déjeuner = breakfast and le dîner = lunch. Midi (noon) is understood everywhere."
- `regionalNotesEnglish[0]`, replace with: "France: petit-déjeuner, déjeuner, dîner. Quebec, Belgium and Switzerland: déjeuner (breakfast), dîner (lunch), souper (supper). Say à midi to avoid confusion."

**3. L6: `vivre` breaks the lesson's own objective.** The objective is "regular verbs." The draft uses irregular *vivre*, then admits that *habiter* is the everyday verb. *Habiter* is a regular -er verb, more natural, and matches the objective. The current phrase also uses inversion (*Où vivez-vous ?*), which contradicts L5's note that intonation is the standard spoken form.
- In `verbs[2]`, set `infinitiveFrench` to "habiter" and `exampleFrench` to "Vous habitez où ?". The English stays "to live" / "Where do you live?".
- Change `words[Where do you live?].fr` to "Vous habitez où ?". Change its note to: "Habiter is the everyday verb for where you live. Vivre also means to live (je vis), but it is irregular."
- In `grammarFocusEnglish`, replace "irregular vivre" with "habiter (regular; j'habite with elision)".
- In `grammarTeachingEnglish`, replace the *vivre* paradigm with: "Habiter is regular: j'habite, tu habites, il/elle/on habite, nous habitons, vous habitez, ils/elles habitent. The h is silent, so je becomes j'."
- Replace `regionalNotesEnglish[2]` to match.

**4. L4: two key utterances use grammar the lesson never teaches.**
- **(a) `speakingFrenchOpening`:** the model answer for "what is missing" uses *Il manque des clous*. *Il manque* is never taught. Either:
  - change the line to "Il n'y a plus de clous sur le toit.", which uses only taught structures, or
  - add to `grammarTeachingEnglish` and `grammarFocusEnglish`: "Il manque + noun = … is missing: il manque des clous. Like il y a, it does not change."
- **(b) `words[You have to wear the harness.].fr`:** this uses *Vous devez*, but *devoir* is never taught. The lesson's own structure for "have to" is *il faut*. Change it to "Il faut porter le harnais." In the note, add "Il faut = you have to / it is necessary." Keep the existing employer and local-rules disclaimer.

**5. L5 and L6: the contraction `au` is used everywhere but never taught.** It appears in L5's `reading.titleFrench`, `reading.target[1]` and `speakingFrenchOpening`, and in L6's `reading.target[1]`. F1–F3 never teach it either.
- Append to L5 `grammarTeachingEnglish`: "À + le becomes au: au chantier. À + les becomes aux. À la and à l' do not change: à la maison, à l'échelle."
- Add "à + le = au" to `grammarFocusEnglish`.
