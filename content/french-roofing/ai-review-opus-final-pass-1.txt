## What is still wrong with the numbers (material)

The L2 objective and grammar focus both say "French numbers 0 to 100". The teaching text does not deliver that:

- **Missing numbers:** 40 quarante, 50 cinquante and 100 cent are never given, although 100 is the stated end of the range.
- **1–16 named but not shown:** the text says "1–16 as base words" but never lists un through seize, so the learner has nothing to learn from.
- **Wrong rule for quatre-vingts (new error from the edit):** the text says "the s of quatre-vingts drops before dix." The s actually drops before any number that follows it: quatre-vingt-un, quatre-vingt-trois, quatre-vingt-dix. As written, a learner will spell quatre-vingts-trois.
- **No warning about 81 and 91:** the text teaches et in vingt et un and soixante et onze but never says that 81 and 91 take no et (quatre-vingt-un, quatre-vingt-onze). A learner will reasonably produce *quatre-vingt et un*, which is wrong.
- **No hyphen rule:** nothing says compounds are hyphenated (vingt-deux, trente-cinq).

Suggested replacement for the numbers part of L2 `grammarTeachingEnglish`. It also removes the "Teach" wording:

> Numbers 0–16 are single words: zéro, un, deux, trois, quatre, cinq, six, sept, huit, neuf, dix, onze, douze, treize, quatorze, quinze, seize. 17–19 build on dix: dix-sept, dix-huit, dix-neuf. Tens: vingt, trente, quarante, cinquante, soixante. Other numbers are joined with a hyphen (vingt-deux, trente-cinq). For 21, 31, 41, 51 and 61, use et instead: vingt et un. 71 is soixante et onze. 70–79 count on from soixante: soixante-dix, soixante-douze. 80 is quatre-vingts; the s drops when another number follows: quatre-vingt-un (no et), quatre-vingt-cinq. 90–99 run from quatre-vingt-dix to quatre-vingt-dix-neuf; 91 is quatre-vingt-onze, again with no et. 100 is cent.

Keep the rest of the field from "For clock time say il est sept heures…" onward unchanged.
