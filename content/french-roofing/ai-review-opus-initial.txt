**M2 — L1 `grammarTeachingEnglish`: the silent-letter rule is wrong.**
- The draft says "toit, parle, vite, and Comment lose their final consonant or e". In `vite` and `parle` the t and l are pronounced; only the e is silent. In `dit-on` the t is pronounced too.
- Fix: "Final consonants are often silent (toit, Comment). A final e is silent but the consonant before it is said (vite, parle). In dit-on the t is sounded and links to on."

**M3 — L1 `words[9].noteEnglish`: the speech claim is inaccurate.**

`★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────`
- Spanish's ser/estar split doesn't carry over to French. The real "be" traps in French are être vs avoir (j'ai froid), être vs faire (il fait froid), and c'est vs il est. A lesson ported from Spanish should teach those instead.
- When adjectives change for the feminine, French sometimes changes the sound (prêt → prête) and sometimes only the spelling (sûr → sûre). Teaching all of them as silent builds wrong pronunciation early.
