## Lesson 28

1. **Speaking cue gives away the answer.** The partner says "The broom is in picture A. What is in that picture?" and the learner answers "There is a broom."
   - Proposal: partner "What is in picture A?" → learner "There is a broom."
2. **"These are nails." answers the wrong question.** "What is in picture B?" (and « Qu'y a-t-il… ») does not elicit "These are…".
   - Proposal: "What are these in picture B?" / « Que sont ces objets sur l'image B ? ».
3. **The opening uses an untaught form and the goal names the wrong actor.** "Where is the broom?" is first explained in lesson 30. The goal says "Locate the broom picture", but the partner does the locating. Form 1 ("The … is here") and "trash" never appear in the exchange.
4. **Objective overclaims.** Gutter, cleanup and debris appear only as bare words, and "The trash is here." is a location sentence.
   - Proposal: "…as words and in location, presence and identification sentences", or narrow the list.
5. **Grammar explanations differ.** The French adds the uncountable rule for trash/debris; the English omits it. Form 1 should read "The [singular or uncountable noun] is here."
6. **reader:1 French drops "already" and "still".**
   - Proposal: « La toiture est déjà terminée, mais il reste encore le nettoyage. »
7. **Terminology glosses go beyond the source cards.**
   - Card 5: the gloss "magnet (magnetic sweep)" imports card 12's sense, the only option is the narrowed « l'aimant de ramassage », and generic « l'aimant » is not listed.
   - Card 8: the gloss "yard / patio" mixes the Spanish word into the English meaning.
8. **Hold reasons are incomplete.**
   - reader:8 cites only yard but also depends on the held dumpster.
   - The review note on gutter-check commands omits reader:2.
   - Dumpster rows carry downspout text.
