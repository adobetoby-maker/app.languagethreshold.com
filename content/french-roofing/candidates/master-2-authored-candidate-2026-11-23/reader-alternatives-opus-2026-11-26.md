# Language critique: Master 1–3 French Reader alternatives (35 rows)

"No correction" below means I found no language error. It is not an approval, and no hold is resolved. The corrected wording is French text only. It makes no claim about installation, tools or safety.

## Applies to several rows: semicolon spacing

Rows 1:13, 2:14, 3:6, 3:7, 3:11 and 3:16 use `;` with no space before it. The same rows do put a space before `:` and `?`. French typography requires the same spacing before `;`, so use whatever space character the files already use before `:`.

## Alignment notes that become inaccurate

- **1:3 and 1:6:** neither note mentions the added actor. With the corrections, describe "va" as rendered by "vient" / "se pose".
- **1:6:** "keeps the causal reason" is true, but the note should also say that "más agua" is a quantity of water.
- **2:5:** the note claims "impersonal se as on", but the first clause was passive. It is accurate only after the correction.
- **3:10:** the note doesn't mention the added "alors".
- **3:16:** the note names "revisez-le" as the vous command; it should say "vérifiez-le".

## Source, product, role and register uncertainty (separate from the corrections)

- **Regional and trade terms still to confirm:**
  - avant-toit (Québec usage) vs égout / rive d'égout (France)
  - larmier vs bordure d'égouttement
  - platelage vs voligeage
  - grenier vs combles
  - bande de départ vs rang de départ: Spanish uses "hilera" for both the starter strip and the courses, and French loses that link
  - fente vs découpe
  - ligne de craie vs cordeau traceur
  - tuyau vs boyau (Québec)
  - sceller, which is acceptable in Québec usage
- **1:6:** the English source names the product "ice and water shield", but the Spanish says only "membrana impermeable".
- **1:12:** the singular "el caballete" becomes the plural "les bardeaux de faîtage", which adds a shingle product. Ridge (cumbrera) and ridge cap (caballete) must stay distinct in French.
- **1:13:** "la ventilación" (generic) is rendered as "l’évent" (a device).
- **2:9:** Spanish "hasta que es tarde" has no "demasiado", but English "too late" does. "Trop" follows the English, so a reviewer needs to settle the intensity.
- **3:5 and 3:6:** Spanish "muy alto" is closer to "very high". "Trop haut" follows the English "too high".
- **3:4:** the referent of "Clávalo" is unclear: it could be the shingle or the nail ("teja" is feminine but "lo" is masculine). "Cloue-le" assumes the shingle.
- **3:16 and context row 15:** keeping "Don" conflicts with the archived context's "M. Ernesto". Treat it as a role and honorific decision, not a grammar error.
