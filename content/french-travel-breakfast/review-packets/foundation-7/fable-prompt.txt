## 1. French false meanings and register

| Idx | Candidate | Issue | Proposal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Card 1 | `quoi ?` | On its own, *quoi ?* is informal, close to "huh?/what?". It is also not the form used in the deck's own questions (card 11 uses *qu'est-ce que*, R2 uses *quel*). This is a false-friend risk for the "what?" concept. | `que ? / qu'est-ce que ?` with a note: *quoi* is used alone or after a preposition. |
| Card 6 | `comment ?` | On its own, this also means "pardon?". That is a minor ambiguity. | Keep it, but add a note that *comment* = how, and that *Comment s'appelle…* is the idiom for "what is it called". |
| Card 7 | `pourquoi ?` | The source note (por qué vs porque) is dropped and not adapted. | Add a note: *pourquoi* (why) vs *parce que* (because). |
| Card 8 | `quel ? / lequel ?` | This face has two answers (adjective vs pronoun), and gender agreement is not shown. | `quel(le) ? / lequel (laquelle) ?` with a note: *quel* + noun or *être*; *lequel* stands alone. |
| Card 16 / R1 | `Qui nous sert ?` | This is understandable. *Qui s'occupe de nous ?* is the more natural phrase in hospitality. | Reviewer's choice. Whichever is chosen, keep card 16, R1 and R2 consistent. |
| R2 | `Je vous sers.` | It can read as "I'm serving you (food)", which is acceptable. *Je m'occupe de vous* is more idiomatic. | Mirror the R1 choice. |
| R4 | `pourquoi ne pas l'essayer ?` | *Essayer* is generic "try". For food, *goûter* is the better fit. The impersonal infinitive also drops the source's *ustedes* address. | `pourquoi ne le goûtez-vous pas ?` |
| Card 9 / R6 | `petit-déjeuner inclus` | This is correct. *compris* is equally common on hotel rate cards. | Optional: `inclus (compris)`. |

## 3. Question-word face ambiguity (qué vs cuál)

French collapses the qué/cuál distinction, and the candidate never says so.

- **Card 1** (qué) is shown as `quoi ?`, but its Reader instance (**R2**, *¿qué café?*) is rendered `Quel café`.
- **Card 8** (cuál) is shown as `quel ?`, and its Reader instance (**R3**, card 15) is also `Quel`.
- As a result, R2 and R3 both show *quel*, while the cards show two different faces. A learner cannot tell from the face alone which source word is meant.

**Proposal:** add a note to card 1 and card 8: *Devant un nom, ¿qué? et ¿cuál? se traduisent tous deux par « quel ».* Also tag R2 → card 1 and R3 → card 8 in the alignment notes.

## 6. Missing verb paradigms

The source has five paradigms with five slots each. French needs six persons, because *vous* is distinct from *ils*. The mapping of the source slot `ellos_ustedes` has to be decided: ustedes → vous, ellos → ils.

| Source | French | je / tu / il / nous / vous / ils | Note to adapt |
|---|---|---|---|
| costar | coûter | coûte / coûtes / coûte / coûtons / coûtez / coûtent | Regular. The source's o→ue note does not transfer. Keep the one vs several (coûte/coûtent) point. |
| llamarse | s'appeler | m'appelle / t'appelles / s'appelle / nous appelons / vous appelez / s'appellent | Pronominal. Note the *ll* doubling, which is not a stem change. |
| estar | être | suis / es / est / sommes / êtes / sont | French has no ser/estar split, so the "location" note needs rewording. R8 uses *sont*. |
| atender | servir (or s'occuper de) | sers / sers / sert / servons / servez / servent | This depends on the choice made for card 16 and R1. |
| querer | vouloir | veux / veux / veut / voulons / voulez / veulent | Irregular. Replace the "quieren = ustedes" note with "voulez = vous (formal or plural)". |

The examples should reuse the French card or Reader lines: card 13, card 14, card 12, R2a and R2b.
