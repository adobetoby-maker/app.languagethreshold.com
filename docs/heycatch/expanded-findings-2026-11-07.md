Key findings & insights
D1.1 Hook (5/6, Pass): Positioning was 12/25 last audit, now 20/25: this run reads the fully rendered pages, and the hero carries the positioning. The eyebrow says “Job-specific language training for nurses, foremen, teams, coaches, and missionaries”, the main headline “Practice the conversations your work actually needs.”, and the sub names the mechanism: “Choose role-specific lessons, practice with an AI speaking partner, and rehearse real situations in Field Prep.” Category, named buyers and the bigger goal (use the language while you are still learning it) all sit above the fold. The one deduction: the eyebrow packs five audiences (“teams, coaches, and missionaries”) into one line where the brief's buying contexts are meant to stay separated.
D1.2 Pain match (4/5, Pass): The three role cards hit the right pains in concrete terms: “Rehearse an intake, explain care, and check understanding.” (Medical), “Run a morning brief, give directions, and reinforce safety.” (Construction). The “use it while you are learning it” sub also answers the plateau pain the category voices. The gap: the site never touches the comprehension pain, “Maybe you can recognize vocabulary but still struggle to understand native speakers.” (dev.to quote in the audience research), and never promises understanding of real, fast, native speech.
D1.3 JTBD coverage (3/4, Pass): The functional jobs are covered almost verbatim: rehearsing the intake conversation, running the morning brief, and Field Prep roleplay map directly to the top ICPs' functional jobs in the audience research. The emotional and social jobs (stop feeling helpless pointing and gesturing; be seen by the crew or patients as someone who made the effort) are unnamed on the fetched pages.
D1.4 Objection handling (2/3, Partial, needs-user-verification): No FAQ section or objection-handling copy appears on any page we were given; the likely concerns (clinical review status of the medical content, working alongside interpreters, time to a usable conversation) are answered nowhere. The audience research itself states no verified objections exist for these buyers yet (“This field needs the planned interview round”), so this score is provisional pending real buyer interviews. What the pages do handle honestly is the billing risk: “Cancel during your free trial to avoid the first subscription charge.”
D1.5 Desired-result visualization (2/3, Partial): The hero itself is text-only in the markup we read: no product screenshot or image element sits in the hero section. The working product is visible below the hero (a live bilingual Reader with clickable words and audio buttons), which is a real demonstration, but its default passage is travel (“Viajes: Comprar un Billete de Tren”), not the medical intake or morning brief the hero promises, so the ICP's own after-state is not shown.
D1.6 Audience language (4/4, Pass): The visible copy uses the audience's exact vocabulary: “morning brief”, “intake”, “role-specific”, “Field Prep”, “crew” all appear on the fetched pages and all sit in the research's use-list. None of the banned framings (“Zero Accident Spanish”, “interpreter replacement”, “guaranteed fluency”) appears anywhere on the pages we read. No vocabulary gap worth naming was found.
