Quick wins

#### Fix the structured-data price that tells machines the product is free

*   **Where:** The structured data block in the served head of the homepage and pricing page

*   **Why (FIT, D5.5):** Both fetched pages publish a schema offer with price 0 while the pricing page sells plans from $19/month to $249/year; an AI assistant reading that data will answer that Language Threshold is free.

*   **What:** Replace the single zero-price offer with the real tiers: Individual $19/month or $149/year, Family $35/month or $249/year, team plans from $99/month for five seats.

*   **Hypothesis:** If the structured data carries the real prices, AI assistants and rich results will stop describing the product as free, and price-aware referrals should reach the trial rather than bounce.

*   **Target metric:** Share of AI-assistant and referral sessions that reach the pricing page (direction: up), over 60 days

*   **Effort:** Low

#### Align the Junior Linguist copy and link with the brief

*   **Where:** Pricing page, Junior Linguist box at the bottom

*   **Why (FIT):** The page says the kids product is for ages 4-12 and links to juniorlinguist.vercel.app, while the brief sets ages 7-14 and names juniorlinguist.com; a parent comparing the two sees a different product than the founder describes.

*   **What:** Confirm the true age range with the product owner, update the card copy to the brief's 7-14 if that is correct, and point the button at juniorlinguist.com.

*   **Hypothesis:** If the kids product copy and destination match the founder's stated product, family-plan evaluators will not hit a contradictory offer at the moment of decision.

*   **Target metric:** Click-through from the pricing page to the Junior Linguist site (direction: up), over 4 weeks

*   **Effort:** Low

#### Point each page's canonical tag at itself

*   **Where:** Served head of /pricing, /vs/clinicalingo, /vs/duolingo, /blog, /team and /support

*   **Why (D5.1, D5.2, D5.3):** Every subpage fetched carries a canonical tag pointing at the homepage root, which tells search engines those pages are duplicates of the homepage; the pricing page and both comparison pages are effectively asking Google not to rank them. The homepage also renders a second main headline, “The Arena”, inside the Language Match overlay.

*   **What:** Emit a per-page canonical URL so /pricing canonicals to /pricing and each /vs page to itself, and demote the overlay's Arena headline from h1.

*   **Hypothesis:** If each page canonicals to itself, the pricing and comparison pages become eligible to rank for their own searches instead of consolidating into the homepage.

*   **Target metric:** Organic impressions for the pricing and /vs page URLs in Search Console (direction: up), over 60 days

*   **Effort:** Low

#### Put a real role-specific product visual in the hero

*   **Where:** Homepage hero, beside or below the main headline

*   **Why (D1.5, D2.1, D5.5):** The hero is text-only in the markup we read, and the working product visible below shows a travel passage about buying a train ticket, not the medical intake or morning brief the hero promises, so the buyer never sees their own conversation being practiced.

*   **What:** Add a screenshot or short looping capture of an actual Field Prep medical-intake or morning-brief session, using a real working flow as the brief requires, with descriptive alt text naming what it shows.

*   **Hypothesis:** If the hero shows the buyer's own work conversation being practiced, first-pass comprehension and hero call-to-action clicks rise for that role's traffic.

*   **Target metric:** Hero call-to-action click-through rate on medical and construction referral traffic (direction: up), over 4 weeks

*   **Effort:** Medium

#### Add the recommended badge and a value anchor to pricing

*   **Where:** Pricing page, plan cards

*   **Why (D4.3):** No tier carries a Most popular or Recommended label, and the price is never framed against an alternative cost; the annual plan is only distinguished by a colored border, so the visitor evaluates from a blank slate.

*   **What:** Label Individual annual as Recommended in visible text, and add one anchoring line near the price such as “About $12.42/month, billed annually - daily practice for the cost of a work lunch.”

*   **Hypothesis:** If one tier is visibly recommended and the price is anchored to a familiar cost, more buyers will choose the annual plan instead of defaulting to monthly.

*   **Target metric:** Annual share of new Individual subscriptions (direction: up), over 4 weeks

*   **Effort:** Low

#### Collect and publish the first named customer stories

*   **Where:** A new testimonials section on the homepage below the role cards, echoed on the pricing page

*   **Why (D3.2, D3.1):** No testimonial appears on any page we read, and the brief confirms none are verified yet; the single strongest early-stage trust mechanic is completely absent from a site selling to skeptical professionals.

*   **What:** Ask the first paying learners (a nurse, a foreman, a mission coordinator) for a one-line quote with first name, photo and role, and publish 3-5 with the specific situation each practiced, such as an intake or a morning brief.

*   **Hypothesis:** If real named users with roles and specific outcomes appear on the page, pricing-page visitors who distrust app marketing claims will be more likely to start the trial.

*   **Target metric:** Pricing page to trial-start conversion rate (direction: up), over 4 weeks after publishing

*   **Effort:** Medium

#### Add a real signup counter with avatars

*   **Where:** Homepage, under the primary call to action

*   **Why (D3.1):** No usage or learner count appears anywhere in the fetched markup; a specific number with visual texture is the cheapest quantity proof a young product can ship.

*   **What:** Build a counter component wired to real signup data (learners trained, lessons completed, or documents translated) plus a small avatar grid of consenting users; ship it only once the number is real and non-trivial, per the brief's no-invented-numbers rule.

*   **Hypothesis:** If a live count with real avatars sits near the call to action, homepage visitors will extend enough trust to start the trial.

*   **Target metric:** Homepage call-to-action click-through rate (direction: up), over 4 weeks

*   **Effort:** Medium

#### Add a dated third-party proof strip

*   **Where:** Homepage, below the testimonials section

*   **Why (D3.5):** No review badge, rating, press mention or community link appears on any page we read, so the site surfaces zero external validation in a category where buyers explicitly distrust marketing claims.

*   **What:** Build a strip component that carries real, dated third-party signals as they land: a Product Hunt discussion link, an app-store rating with review count, or a newsletter or community mention. Leave it unrendered rather than fabricated until the first real signal exists.

*   **Hypothesis:** If dated external validation is visible near the decision point, trial-start conversion improves among the marketing-skeptical buyers this audience research describes.

*   **Target metric:** Pricing page to trial-start conversion rate (direction: up), over 8 weeks

*   **Effort:** Low

#### Publish an FAQ that answers the real objections

*   **Where:** A new FAQ section on the homepage or pricing page

*   **Why (D1.4, D5.5):** No FAQ appears on the pages we read, and the buyer's likely concerns (what is and is not clinically reviewed, working alongside interpreters, time to a usable conversation, how team seats work) are answered nowhere on the site.

*   **What:** Add 6-8 questions answered honestly in the brief's voice: the clinical review status of the medical content stated plainly, how the tool supports rather than replaces qualified interpreters, how long to a basic work conversation, and how the five-seat team plan works. Mark the section up with FAQ structured data.

*   **Hypothesis:** If the objections are surfaced and answered in the product's own honest voice, hesitant professional buyers will progress further down the page toward the trial.

*   **Target metric:** FAQ-to-pricing click-through rate (direction: up), over 4 weeks

*   **Effort:** Medium

#### Write a third comparison page and link the set from the home page

*   **Where:** A new /vs page plus homepage footer links to all comparison pages

*   **Why (D5.3, D1.4):** Two comparison pages exist (vs Duolingo, vs ClinicaLingo) but the homepage navigation we read links to neither, and the closest workplace rival in the fresh research, CanopyLearn, has no page; full marks need three or more reachable pages.

*   **What:** Add a Language Threshold vs CanopyLearn page in the same four-row table format, honestly hedged like the existing pages, and link all three from the homepage footer.

*   **Hypothesis:** If the comparison set is complete and reachable in one click, comparison-intent search visitors will land on a page that moves them to the trial.

*   **Target metric:** Organic entrances to the /vs pages (direction: up), over 60 days

*   **Effort:** Medium

#### Complete the founder trust block

*   **Where:** Pricing page founder card and the app footer

*   **Why (D3.3):** The pricing page names Toby Anderton and links “Why I built Language Threshold”, but no photo or professional profile link appears on the pages we read, and the founder is absent from the app's own footer.

*   **What:** Add the founder photo and a LinkedIn or X profile link to the pricing founder card, and surface the same link in the app footer.

*   **Hypothesis:** If a face and a verifiable profile sit beside the story, buyers skeptical of faceless language apps will extend more trust to the trial.

*   **Target metric:** Founder-page visits originating from the pricing page (direction: up), over 4 weeks

*   **Effort:** Low

Bigger bets

#### Give marketing visitors a guided scroll before the raw app

*   **Where:** The app.languagethreshold.com homepage structure, between hero and app workspace

*   **Why (D2.3, D2.1, D2.2):** The fetched homepage places the full learning app, a sidebar of 21 equally weighted tool buttons and a live Reader, directly under the hero with no staged how-it-works or proof sections between them, and the hero offers three same-weight calls to action.

*   **What:** Insert a marketing scroll between hero and app: a three-step how-it-works using real product frames, the proof section, and a pricing teaser, before the workspace. Collapse the 21-item sidebar behind a grouped menu for signed-out visitors and make “Start the 7-day free trial” the visually dominant hero action.

*   **Hypothesis:** If a first-time visitor gets a guided narrative before the raw tool, homepage-to-pricing progression for paid and search traffic will rise against the current single-screen layout.

*   **Target metric:** Homepage to pricing-page progression rate (direction: up), over 4 weeks

*   **Effort:** High
